What this is

Not a webinar. Not a podcast.
A peer conversation, in public.

Fusion Conversations is a monthly series where two practitioners — building at the intersection of technology, Islamic thought, and civilizational purpose — think out loud together. No slides. No keynote. Just tafakkur: two cognitions producing a third.

The format

50 minutes of structured thinking + open floor

Four segments: check-in, build exchange (what each of us is shipping), the fusion thread (where the ideas collide), and a mutual ask. Ends with a concrete follow-up — no NATO (No Action Talk Only).

The philosophy

Peer learning as a more humbling model than the guru

No single lead figure. Firdaus and Sha come as equals with different labs, different languages, and different vantage points. The goal is wisdom transfer — not performance. Ship conversations, not content.

Who it's for

Muslim professionals who build things and want to think clearly about why

Engineers, researchers, educators, founders who feel the pull between productivity and meaning — and want a space that takes both seriously. Bahasa Malaysia and English speakers both welcome.

The series

Monthly, recurring — ~15th of each month

Session 01 launches the format. Future sessions may bring in guests from the Rehlah Intensive (Indonesia) audience and beyond. Each session is self-contained; the series compounds over time.

Session structure

How the hour runs

0–10 min
Check-in

Where are we, actually?

A brief personal check-in — not pleasantries, but a genuine reading of where each person is arriving from. Sets the register for honest conversation rather than performance.

10–25 min
Build exchange

What are you shipping?

Each speaker shares what they are actively building. Session 01: Sha on Hermes governance architecture + AI safety experiments + Irori. Firdaus on Kakehashi automation stack + Firdaus Labs + Bahasa Melayu content strategy. The audience sees real work, not polished retrospectives.

25–45 min
The fusion thread

Where the ideas collide

The core of the session. Session 01 threads: tafakkur as the missing link between AI tools and meaningful output (al-Ghazālī's two-cognitions-make-a-third). Civilizational thinking vs. complacency. The privilege and responsibility of insan. Inspirational adults as infrastructure. Hayatan ṭayyibah — the life worth living — and what sa'ā (striving recognized by Allah) actually demands of a Muslim professional.

45–55 min
Mutual ask + close

What do we actually need from each other?

Each speaker names one concrete ask — a resource, a connection, feedback, a decision. Open floor for audience questions. Closes with one follow-up commitment from each speaker, stated publicly. No NATO.

Session 01 — themes on the table

What we will actually think through

These emerged from a 90-minute pre-session between Firdaus and Sha. They are not fixed — the conversation will go where it goes.

01

Tafakkur as the missing layer

Al-Ghazālī defines tafakkur as bringing two cognitions together to produce a third — new insight that wasn't in either one alone. Without it, AI tools stay informational. With it, they become meaningful. How do we build tafakkur into our workflows — not just our rhetoric?

Al-Ghazālī — Iḥyāʾ, on reflection

02

Civilizational thinking vs. complacency

Hamka's warning: mere survival is animalistic. The ummah's call is khilafah — not just endurance but stewardship across time. Jewish and Ottoman examples of long-horizon thinking. What does a 30-year horizon look like for a Muslim professional in 2026?

Hamka · Ottoman historiography

03

Inspirational adults as infrastructure

Sheikh Sha'rawi on Anas bin Malik — shaped not just by the Prophet ﷺ but by being inside that ecosystem. Inspirational adults ripple outward to families, schools, strangers. Sha and Firdaus as peer models rather than gurus. What does that responsibility look like in practice?

Sheikh Mutawalli Sha'rawi

04

Hayatan ṭayyibah & the kasaba / sa'ā distinction

Two Quranic roots for striving — kasaba (acquisition) and sa'ā (purposeful effort recognized by Allah). The life worth living is not just productive; it is recognized. What does sa'ayuhum mashkūrā demand of how we spend our working hours?

Quran 53:39 · 20:15

05

Having your own lab

Firdaus on observing Japan: many products for the same need because each maker's uniqueness comes from how they were raised and what they were given to see. A lab normalizes failure so the rare success can emerge. What does running your own lab — as a Muslim, in Malaysia — actually look like?

Firdaus Labs · CetaLabs

06

Language as a civilizational choice

Firdaus writes in Bahasa Malaysia deliberately — to extract and deliver local wisdom. Sha builds in English first for sustainability, then extends. Za'ba and Dr. Afifi Akiki as reference points. Is the language we build in a strategic decision or a values decision? Can it be both?

Za'ba · Dr. Afifi Akiki

"Knowledge is the arrival of meaning to the soul. Without tafakkur, AI tools stay informational and never become meaningful."

Al-Ghazālī — paraphrased in session · Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm al-Dīn
